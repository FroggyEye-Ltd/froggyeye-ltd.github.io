The short version

Your photo never leaves your device. Passport Photo Studio has no servers and no accounts, and we — Froggy Eye — receive no data about you at all.

Two components built by Apple and Google are bundled inside the app, and both of them do use the network. One checks whether you have paid; the other is the face-detection library, which sends Google its own diagnostics. Neither sends your photograph. The detail is below, because "we collect nothing" would be easier to say than it is to defend, and you should be able to check.

What the app does with your photos

When you take a photo or choose one from your library, the app reads it, detects the face on your device using Google's ML Kit — an offline library whose model is bundled with the app — crops and scales it to the document specification you selected, and shows you the result.

That processing happens in the app, on your phone. No image is uploaded anywhere, by us or by anyone else. The face detection runs against a model file inside the app; there is no server involved in it and none of your photograph is transmitted.

The image is held in memory while you are looking at it. The source photo — the copy the camera or the photo picker hands the app — is deleted as soon as the app has finished with it, and anything left behind by a crash is cleared the next time the app starts. The only copies that persist are the ones you explicitly save to your own photo gallery.

Face data

Because the app detects a face in your photo, this section states exactly what happens to face data, in the terms privacy laws and the app stores use.

What face data is processed. To centre and size the crop, the app runs Google ML Kit's on-device face detection over your photo. That produces a face bounding box and the positions of facial landmarks (such as the eyes) as coordinates in the image. The app reduces those coordinates to simple geometric measurements — face height, eye-line position — and uses them for two things only: cropping the photo to the document specification you chose, and running the compliance checks shown on the result screen.

No face data is collected, stored, or shared. The detection runs entirely on your device against a model bundled inside the app. The bounding box, landmark coordinates and derived measurements exist only in the app's working memory while your photo is being processed and displayed; they are discarded when processing finishes and are never written to disk, never included in any saved file, never transmitted off your device, and never shared with us or with any third party. ML Kit's diagnostic reports to Google (described below) contain no face data and no part of your image.

No recognition, no identification, no profiling. The app performs face detection — "where is a face in this picture" — not face recognition. It creates no faceprint or biometric template, cannot identify anyone, does not compare faces across photos, and builds no database of faces.

Retention: none. Face data is retained only for the seconds your photo is being processed and viewed, in memory, on your device. There is nothing for us to retain — we never receive it. The only face-related thing that can persist is the finished photograph itself, and only if you choose to save it to your own photo library.

What uses the network

The app makes no requests of its own. It has no server to talk to and contains no code that contacts one. Two bundled components do use the network:

  • The store's in-app purchase library (Apple's or Google's), to check whether you own Passport Photo Studio Pro. It sends no photo and nothing about what you do in the app.
  • Google's ML Kit, the offline face-detection library. The detection itself runs entirely on your device against a model bundled inside the app — your photo is never sent anywhere. ML Kit does, however, send Google its own diagnostic and usage data: a non-advertising device identifier, performance and error information, and counts of which of its features ran. We do not receive that data, we cannot see it, we cannot switch it off, and it contains no part of your photograph. Google processes it under its own privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

What is stored on your device

  • The last document specification you chose, so the app opens where you left off.
  • Whether you have purchased Passport Photo Studio Pro.

Both are stored in the app's own private storage on your device. Uninstalling the app removes them. Neither is transmitted anywhere.

Permissions

  • Camera — so you can take a photo in the app. Optional; you can import an existing photo instead.
  • Photo library (read) — so you can choose an existing photo to crop.
  • Photo library (add) — so the finished photo and print sheet can be saved back to your library.

The app asks for nothing else. It does not request location, contacts, the microphone, or background access.

Purchases

Passport Photo Studio Pro is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Apple's App Store or Google Play. We never see or store your payment details. The stores tell the app only whether the purchase exists; the record of it lives in your store account, which is why "Restore purchase" works on a new device.

Apple and Google process that transaction under their own privacy policies:

  • Apple: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/
  • Google: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Analytics, advertising and tracking

We do not track you. The app contains no advertising SDK, does not use your advertising identifier, does not ask for permission to track you across other apps or websites, and builds no profile of you. Nothing you do in the app is reported to us — we have added no analytics and no crash reporting of our own, so we could not see it even if we wanted to.

The one qualification, and it is the reason this section is not simply "there are none": Google's ML Kit carries its own usage-analytics client, described in the section above. It reports to Google, not to us, about how the library itself performed. It is not used for advertising and it is not linked to your identity, but it is analytics, and calling the app analytics-free would not be true.

Children

We collect no data from anyone, including children. The app has no accounts, no social features and no advertising.

Your rights

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to disclose, correct, export or delete. Everything the app knows about you is on your device and under your control — deleting the app deletes it.

For the diagnostic data ML Kit sends Google, Google is the data controller and your rights are exercised through them, under the policy linked above.
