4. Where the data comes from, and its limits

Food Score builds its results from sources we do not control:

  • Open Food Facts, a public, community-maintained product database, supplies barcode lookups and the nutrition values that come with them. Entries are contributed by volunteers and may be incomplete, out of date, mismatched to the product in your hand, or simply wrong.
  • AI food recognition identifies food from a photo and estimates its nutrition. It is a statistical guess. It can misidentify a food entirely, and its nutrition figures are approximations, not measurements.

Manufacturers also reformulate products and change pack sizes without changing the barcode. The label on the packaging is always the authoritative source — check it rather than relying on what the app shows. Scores, calorie counts, daily totals and alternatives are provided for general information and are not guaranteed to be accurate, complete or current.
